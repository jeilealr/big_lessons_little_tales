Once upon a time, a great lion was sleeping beneath a tree when a little mouse accidentally ran across his paw.

The lion woke with a growl and caught the mouse beneath his paw.

The mouse trembled, but the lion looked at the tiny creature and realized there was no reason to hurt him.

“You frightened me,” said the lion, “but you meant no harm. Go on your way.”

The mouse was surprised. He had expected anger, but instead the lion showed him kindness.

“Thank you,” said the mouse. “I hope one day I can show someone the same kindness.”

Some days later, the lion became trapped in a hunter’s net. He pulled and struggled, but the ropes only tightened around him.

The mouse heard his roars and hurried toward the sound.

When he saw the lion trapped, he did not stop to think about whether the lion deserved his help or whether he owed him anything.

He simply saw someone in trouble.

“I think I can help,” said the mouse.

He began to gnaw through the ropes with his sharp little teeth. It took time, but at last the net loosened and the lion stepped free.

The lion looked at the mouse with amazement.

“You helped me even though you did not have to.”

The mouse smiled.

“You were kind to me when you did not have to either.”

The lion thought about this for a moment.

Perhaps kindness was not something to be repaid like a debt.

Perhaps it was something that could simply be passed on.

And from then on, the lion and the mouse remembered that being strong sometimes means choosing gentleness, and being small never means being powerless.

**Moral:** Kindness does not create a debt. It creates more kindness.
